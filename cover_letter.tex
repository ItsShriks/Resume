Robots only get smarter when the data they learn from is collected carefully, on hardware that is set up and running reliably. I am finishing my M.Sc. in Autonomous Systems at Hochschule Bonn-Rhein-Sieg and would like to bring that hands-on experience to your teleoperation and AI data-collection work in Aachen.

\vspace{4pt}
What I would bring to the role:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2.5pt, topsep=2pt]

    \item \textbf{Robot setup and bring-up:} On a real Neobotix MMO-700 with a UR5e arm and Robotiq gripper, I wrote the ROS~2 bring-up and hardware interfaces for the base, arm, gripper, LiDARs, RealSense L515 and IMU, so the whole system starts on boot without manual steps. At Sakar Robotics I owned hardware commissioning across \textbf{7 robotic platforms}, 2 of them delivered end-to-end.

    \item \textbf{Calibration and troubleshooting during sessions:} With the b-it bots RoboCup@Home team I calibrate RGB-D, LiDAR and odometry on the Toyota HSR and debug hardware-software problems in rosbag, RViz and Foxglove Studio. My person-following there reaches a \textbf{95\% tracking success rate}, and our pointing-gesture detection helped the team complete \textbf{Help Me Carry} at RoboCup German Open 2025. As an intern at SSIG I calibrated Tool Center Points on ABB industrial arms and verified sensors.

    \item \textbf{Data collection and testing for AI:} For the MMO-700 I built sensor feeds, bounding-box annotations and evaluation scripts to check multi-class detection. As a research assistant at H-BRS I traced sensor timing jitter to its root cause and registered field annotations to LiDAR data at \textbf{98.8\% inliers}, so the training data could be trusted.

    \item \textbf{Documentation and procedures:} I record commissioning results and root causes in structured QA reports, and as Scrum Master of a \textbf{10-member team} at Sakar I kept hardware and software work aligned across disciplines.

\end{itemize}
