Most of my recent work sits where an optimisation problem has to become precise motion on a real robot: hierarchical-QP whole-body control and MPC for mobile manipulation. NEURA's humanoid and quadruped platforms take that problem further, with contacts, balance and loco-manipulation, and I would like to apply for the \clrole{} position. I am finishing my M.Sc. in Autonomous Systems at Hochschule Bonn-Rhein-Sieg and have 4+ years of hands-on robotics.

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Hierarchical QP whole-body control, from MuJoCo to the real robot.} At the Humanoid Robots Lab of the University of Bonn, our mobile manipulator Steve (omnidirectional base + UR5e) has to grasp from a table while the base keeps moving. With a weighted single QP the arm and base tasks fought each other and the gripper drifted by 0.55\,cm RMS (1.90\,cm max) while the base orbits the table. I replaced it with a 9-DOF strict task hierarchy (end effector $>$ base $>$ posture), one constrained least-squares problem per level, using the exact whole-body Jacobian from the MuJoCo model and joint, velocity, acceleration and collision constraints. Each level is warm-started from the one above, and a level that cannot be solved is dropped while the higher levels keep their result. The error dropped to \textbf{0.01\,cm RMS} (0.03\,cm max) at 10--13\,ms per solve. On the real robot I switched the UR5e to velocity commands, which cut figure-8 tracking error from 0.17 to \textbf{0.12\,cm RMS}, calibrated the wrist camera without markers to 0.17\,cm RMS, and Steve now completes full grasps on hardware (\href{https://github.com/ItsShriks/steve_wbc}{github.com/ItsShriks/steve\_wbc}).

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{MPC coupled with TSID.} My master thesis builds the next layer: a visibility-aware MPC planner that feeds a high-frequency TSID controller, so the robot grasps continuously instead of driving, stopping and then reaching; it now grasps in \textbf{about 12\,s}. I wrote the asynchronous ROS~2 bridge in C++17 for trajectory interpolation and real-time rigid-body dynamics checks, and modelled the drives in MuJoCo with gravity / Coriolis feed-forward and an inertia-scaled velocity loop, so the simulated joints track commands like the real robot's.

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Working across disciplines.} At Sakar Robotics I was robotics engineer and Scrum Master of a 10-member engineering team, contributing to seven autonomous platforms and delivering two end-to-end. I owned hardware commissioning and turned CAD assemblies into URDF models for simulation, working between the hardware and software sides of the team.
