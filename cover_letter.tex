\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Shipping software to a real robot.} At the Humanoid Robots Lab of the University of Bonn I am the main developer (137 of 163 commits) of the ROS~2 stack for Steve, a custom mobile manipulator with an omnidirectional base, a UR5e arm, grippers, cameras and two LiDARs. The goal was one codebase that runs unchanged in MuJoCo, Gazebo and on the hardware. I wrote the bring-up so every driver starts hands-off at power-on, with a heartbeat watchdog and LiDARs matched by serial number, and a script that clones, syncs and pushes all package repositories between the lab GitLab and GitHub. Sim-to-real is now confirmed on the real robot, which completes full grasps (\href{https://github.com/ItsShriks/steve_ros2_ws}{github.com/ItsShriks/steve\_ros2\_ws}).

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Reliable releases for offline machines.} At Navilan Solutions, documents had to be read by software running fully offline on low-end PCs. I combined DocTR, Tesseract and EasyOCR into an ensemble with image preprocessing, fine-tuned YOLOv11 on synthetic invoices, and shipped it as C++/Qt desktop applications and Python services with Docker, automated tests and CI/CD quality gates. The result reached \textbf{90--95\% accuracy} on multilingual text and tables on CPUs alone. In the same spirit I packaged my JungleLook research as a tested library with one CLI, Docker and CI that runs on CPU, CUDA and Apple MPS.

\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Running several robots at once.} At Sakar Robotics I was robotics engineer and Scrum Master of a 10-member team, contributing to \textbf{seven autonomous platforms}, two of them delivered end-to-end. I owned hardware commissioning and configuration management there and integrated sensors into ROS / ROS~2 navigation.
