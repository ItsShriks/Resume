## Master Thesis -- Continuous Mobile Manipulation Architecture
When: Apr 2026 -- Present
Where: Master Thesis, H-BRS (on the Steve robot, Humanoid Robots Lab, Uni Bonn)
Link: github.com/ItsShriks/porta_ros2_ws
Tech: ROS 2, C++17, Python, MPC, TSID, MuJoCo, VGN
- Thesis title: "Coupling Visibility-Aware MPC with TSID for Continuous Mobile Manipulation".
- Designing a control architecture that couples a visibility-aware MPC planner with a
  high-frequency TSID controller, so a mobile robot can grasp while it keeps moving.
- Asynchronous ROS 2 bridge and adapters in C++17 for trajectory interpolation, execution logic
  and real-time rigid-body dynamics checks.
- MuJoCo model of the MMO-700 (MPO-700 base, UR5e arm, Robotiq 2F-85 gripper) with a fully
  autonomous drive -> reach -> grasp -> lift pipeline (numerical and differential IK).
- Figure-8 ("arc-8") whole-body-control validation and VGN grasp-planning integration.
STAR:
  Situation: Mobile manipulators usually drive, stop, settle and only then grasp, which is
    slow and fails when the object or the robot moves.
  Task: Thesis goal -- let the robot keep moving and grasp continuously, with the camera
    keeping the object in view.
  Action: Designing a visibility-aware MPC planner coupled to a high-frequency TSID
    controller; wrote the asynchronous ROS 2 bridge and adapters in C++17; built the MuJoCo
    model of the MMO-700 with a full drive -> reach -> grasp -> lift pipeline.
  Result: In progress (thesis due Feb 2027). Working simulation pipeline; the whole-body
    control results are under the Steve entry.
  Missing: Any measured result yet from the MPC + TSID coupling (time to grasp, success rate)?
    The steve_wbc README mentions 36.8 -> 30.8 s to reach the grasp pose (sim, "far" scene)
    when approaching while driving -- is that thesis work, and may it be used?

- Main developer of the full ROS 2 stack: same code runs in MuJoCo, Gazebo and on the real robot
  (sim-to-real confirmed on the real robot, Oct 2026).
- Workspace tooling: package repos mirrored between the lab GitLab and GitHub, one script to

Notes: started as the WS 2025/26 student lab, continued since. Team project; I am the main

steve_hardware_bringup, steve_manipulation and steve_essentials). steve_perception: minor
contributor (8 of 72 commits). steve_command_grounding: NOT my work -- do not claim it.
Use this umbrella entry when there is room for only one Steve entry.
STAR (main story -- whole-body control on Steve):
  Situation: Steve has to grasp objects from a table, including rolling ones, while its
    omnidirectional base keeps moving. With a weighted single QP the arm and base tasks
    fought each other, so the gripper drifted: 0.55 cm RMS / 1.90 cm max error in a
    figure-8 kinematic check while the base orbits the table.
  Task: Build one controller for base + arm that keeps the gripper precise while the base
    moves, and that runs unchanged in MuJoCo, Gazebo and on the real robot.
  Action: Wrote a 9-DOF strict task hierarchy (end effector > base > posture), one
    constrained least-squares problem per level, with the exact whole-body Jacobian from the
    MuJoCo model plus joint, velocity and collision constraints. Added a visibility task that
    keeps the camera on the object, switched the real UR5e to velocity commands, and
    calibrated the wrist camera without markers.
  Result: 0.01 cm RMS / 0.03 cm max error in the same check (vs 0.55 / 1.90 cm), 10--13 ms
    per solve; on the real robot a small figure-8 tracked at 0.12 cm RMS (vs 0.17 cm before);
    camera kept on the object 100% of the time vs 29% without the visibility task (sim);
    camera calibration 0.17 cm RMS over 8 poses.
  Missing: Has the full grasp (incl. a rolling object) worked on the real robot yet? If yes,
    how often (e.g. 4 of 5 tries)? Who used / saw the result (lab demo, supervisor)?

## Steve -- Whole-Body Control & Active Perception
Link: github.com/ItsShriks/steve_wbc
Tech: Python, MuJoCo, QP, MPC, EKF, VGN, ROS 2
- 9-DOF velocity-level whole-body controller (base + UR5e) as a strict task hierarchy
  (end effector > base > posture), one constrained least-squares problem per level, with
  velocity, acceleration, joint-limit and collision constraints (table, obstacles, floor, self).
- Exact whole-body Jacobian from the MuJoCo model. Kinematic check: 0.01 cm RMS / 0.03 cm max
  end-effector error while the base orbits, vs 0.55 / 1.90 cm for a weighted single QP;
  10--13 ms per solve.
- Active perception: drive, detect from the first tick (head + wrist RGB-D), track the object,
  MPC interception of a moving (rolling) object, grasp, lift and hold check.
- Visibility task keeps the camera on the object: 100% of the time vs 29% without it
  (simulation, static ball, 1-view VGN).
- Smooth orbit planner around the table, occlusion map, VGN grasp planning with "try another
  side" exploration, EKF baseline vs least-squares tracker + MPC comparison.
- Real robot: switched the UR5e to velocity commands (quieter, small figure-8 tracked at 0.12 cm
  RMS vs 0.17 cm); marker-free eye-in-hand calibration at 0.17 cm RMS over 8 poses; EM-stop and
  LiDAR protective-field safety stop.

## JungleLook -- Label-free LiDAR Forest Point Cloud Segmentation
When: Oct 2024 -- Sep 2026 (grew out of the H-BRS research assistant job)
Link: github.com/ItsShriks/JungleLook
Tech: Python, PyTorch, PointNet / PointNet++, Open3D, RANSAC, ICP, DBSCAN, Docker, CI
- Turns unlabelled UAV LiDAR clouds into labelled clouds plus a tree-stump inventory without
  manual annotation: geometric features (ground model, height above ground, eigenvalues) make
  pseudo-labels that train PointNet / PointNet++.
- PointNet and PointNet++ written from scratch in pure PyTorch; run on CPU, CUDA and Apple MPS.
- Results: 0.80 validation mIoU (preliminary, vs. pseudo-labels; ground 0.99, other 0.95,
  stump 0.46); annotation-to-LiDAR registration with RANSAC + ICP at 98.8% inliers, 0.11 m RMS.
- Packaged as a library with one CLI, YAML configs, CI, Docker and tests on synthetic forests.
Notes: rule-based stump detection F1 0.31 vs 129 annotated stumps -- only mention with
"limited by the data". NEVER use 15%, 11.8% or 93%.
STAR:
  Situation: Reforestation teams survey clear-cuts with UAV LiDAR to find ground, tree stumps
    and remaining vegetation. The point clouds arrive unlabelled, and hand-labelling millions
    of points per site does not scale.
  Task: Segment the clouds and produce a stump inventory without any manual labels.
  Action: Generated pseudo-labels from geometric features (ground model, height above
    ground, eigenvalues, vertical continuity, DBSCAN); wrote PointNet and PointNet++ from
    scratch in PyTorch; aligned field annotations to the LiDAR map with RANSAC + ICP; packaged
    it all as a tested library with one CLI.
  Result: 0.80 validation mIoU (preliminary, vs. pseudo-labels); 98.8% annotation inliers at
    0.11 m RMS. Showed that stump detection is limited by the data (ground layer ~26 cm thick,
    stumps only 20--40 cm tall), so the limit is measured instead of hidden.
  Missing: Who was it for (project partner, lab, research group)? Did anyone use the output?
